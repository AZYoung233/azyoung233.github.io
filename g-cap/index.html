1 Abstract

While Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in image captioning, existing research primarily focuses on real-world scenarios, leaving surreal, highly stylized, and semantically hybrid virtual-world scenarios significantly underexplored.

We introduce Game Character Captioning, a task for perceiving and describing characters from games and anime. To evaluate it, we establish GC-Bench, a manually annotated benchmark, and propose Graph-F1. Current SOTA LVLMs — including Gemini 3 Pro and GPT-5.1 — struggle to keep their real-world performance, and a clear gap remains between open- and closed-source models.

We close that gap with GC-148K, a large-scale dataset from a specialized synthesis pipeline, and the G-Cap series. G-Cap rivals advanced closed-source models at open-source cost, offering an efficient recipe for industrial-grade production.

2 Motivation & Task

LVLMs excel at captioning real-world images, yet surreal, highly stylized virtual worlds remain largely unexplored — despite real industrial value in content retrieval, skin recommendation and design, and review.

Game Character Captioning is harder than natural captioning: it demands hierarchical clothing structure, fine-grained color and material, and domain garment knowledge.

Q1. How can we evaluate virtual-world captioning rigorously?
Q2. How can we close the open–closed-source gap at industrial cost?

3 GC-Bench

The first benchmark for Game Character Captioning — 186 meticulously human-annotated characters from 20 games across MOBA, FPS, and open-world RPG titles. Captions average ~290 words. We collapse hundreds of fine-grained art subgenres into six visually distinguishable styles so per-class counts stay statistically usable.

Annotation pipeline

Unlike standard caption datasets, GC-Bench uses a generation-based verification loop (typically 3–5 rounds per image):

1

Initial captioning

GPT-5 drafts a caption with the task-specific prompt $P$.

2

Human refinement + knowledge retrieval

Annotators revise for accuracy and completeness, consulting Google Image Search for precise accessory / garment terminology.

3

Generative verification

Nano Banana reconstructs the image from the caption; annotators compare it with the original to catch missing or inconsistent details.

4

Finalization

After visual consistency, global attributes such as camera pose and artistic style are appended.

4 Graph-F1 Metric

Dense captions break NLG metrics and VLM-as-judge. SPICE matches whole triplets too rigidly; CAPTURE can mis-bind attributes to the wrong object; QA-style relation checks drop local subgraph context. Graph-F1 evaluates attributes and relations jointly inside coherent one-hop subgraphs.

Pipeline. Captions → semantic graphs → parent-node alignment → asymmetric subgraph scoring.
1

Graph construction

GPT-5 parses each caption into triplets $(h, r, t)$ and builds directed graphs $G_{\mathrm{gen}}$ and $G_{\mathrm{gt}}$.

2

Parent-node matching

Entities with outgoing edges are embedded by Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B and aligned by cosine similarity — these parents become anchors for subgraph comparison.

3

Asymmetric subgraph comparison

Each parent’s one-hop triplets are concatenated into a local description. Qwen3-Reranker-4B scores precision (is the generated detail supported?) and recall (is the ground truth covered?).

$\mathrm{Precision}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}^p_{\mathrm{gen}}|}\sum_i S_{ij^*}\cdot R_i^{\mathrm{prec}},\quad \mathrm{Recall}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}^p_{\mathrm{gt}}|}\sum_j S_{i^*j}\cdot R_j^{\mathrm{rec}},\quad \mathrm{Graph\text{-}F1}=\dfrac{2PR}{P+R}$

Embedding and reranker run locally (~10 GB GPU, no API cost). Only triplet parsing uses an LLM (~\$0.075 / caption with GPT-5).

5 GC-148K Dataset

To close the open–closed gap we build GC-148K = GC-Real (78K) + GC-Syn (70K), via a Deconstruct–Recombine–Generate Fashion Simulator.

Overall pipeline. (a) Collect, filter, and caption real images. (b) Extract garments, recombine them, and synthesize new pairs.

GC-Real — high-quality real images

Start from ~100K web images. Using DINOv3 we (i) drop any image with cosine similarity $\gt 0.87$ to GC-Bench (anti-leakage) and (ii) greedily deduplicate pairs above $0.94$. Both thresholds were set by manual inspection. The remaining 78K images are densely captioned by Gemini 2.5 Pro.

GC-Syn — Fashion Simulator

Current LVLMs fail on multi-layer clothing. GC-Syn manufactures exactly that structure:

1

Garment extraction

Qwen3-235B-A22B-FP8 parses the 78K GC-Real captions into five body regions — Head, Torso, Arms, Legs, Body Accessories — yielding a library of 559,880 components.

2

Stochastic recombination & refinement

Compatible components are sampled across categories, assembled into a caption, then reviewed by Qwen3 to resolve logical conflicts.

3

Generative synthesis

Nano Banana renders the caption. One of 103 art mediums and a binary gender attribute are sampled per image to keep style and demographics diverse.

7 Analysis

Data ablation: GC-Real × GC-Syn

GC-Real transfers teacher knowledge from Gemini 2.5 Pro. GC-Syn alone barely helps — Nano Banana’s visual distribution shifts under full-parameter SFT. Together they are synergistic: real textures + synthetic clothing hierarchies.

What vs. how: parent match vs. subgraph

Decomposing Graph-F1 shows a consistent pattern: parent-node similarity is high ($\gt 0.89$) — models know what major entities are there — while subgraph descriptions are much weaker. The remaining errors are hallucinated or incomplete local structure, not missing objects.

Decomposed Graph-F1. Coarse entity recognition is strong; fine-grained subgraph descriptions are the bottleneck.

Density vs. length

Information density is $(|V|+|E|)/L$ — semantic-graph size per word. GPT-5.1 is verbose (>500 words) yet low-density (0.49). Qwen3-VL-8B is dense but omits coverage. Gemini-family models and G-Cap sit in the useful middle.

$\mathrm{Density} = \dfrac{|V| + |E|}{L}$
Bubble = Graph-F1. Future captioners should jointly maximize density and coverage rather than chase length alone.

Style-wise Graph-F1

Models are stronger on Chibi / Realistic / Military and weaker on Sci-Fi / Fantasy / Chinese style — common visual distributions are easier than heavily stylized or culturally specific designs. Resolution itself is not monotonic: mid-range images already suffice.

Limitations

  • Limited knowledge of niche fashion terms — uncommon garments are sometimes missed. Mid-training knowledge injection or tool-augmented Agentic RL is a natural next step.
  • Unconventional cuts and patchwork designs remain hard; richer synthetic data for atypical structures should help.

8 Takeaways

  • Task. Game Character Captioning — LVLM captioning for virtual worlds.
  • Benchmark. GC-Bench (186 images) + Graph-F1, the metric best aligned with humans.
  • Data. GC-148K via a Fashion-Simulator synthesis pipeline.
  • Result. G-Cap rivals advanced closed-source LVLMs at lower cost.
In one line: a benchmark, a metric, and an open recipe bringing industrial-grade game-character captioning within reach.
